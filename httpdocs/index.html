Vanakkam · Welcome

A living shrine since 1881

Raised by Tamil labourers who worked the nearby lime kilns, Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple has grown from a humble roadside shrine into one of Singapore's most revered Dravidian temples — a sanctuary of the Divine Mother, Kali, fierce destroyer of evil and tender protector of her devotees.

Today its 18-metre rajagopuram, crowned with some 600 hand-painted deities, rises over Serangoon Road as a conserved national monument — and as a daily refuge for thousands who come to pray, to give thanks, and to be near the Mother.

The Presiding Deity

Veeramakaliamman

வீரமாகாளி — "Kali the Courageous." A fierce manifestation of Shakti, the Mother stands with garland and blade, slaying ignorance and ego, yet turning a mother's compassion toward all who seek her.

Around her gather thirteen further shrines — Vinayagar and Subramaniyar, Periachi the guardian of mothers, Lakshmi Durgai, Bhairavar, the Navagraham, and more — each with its own prayers and offerings.
