Our History

From the lime kilns of Serangoon to a conserved national monument.

Soonambu Kambam Kovil

The lime-village temple

Long before the present towers rose, the Indian labourers of this district worked the lime kilns that lined Serangoon Road. In Tamil they called their place of worship Soonambu Kambam Kovil — the "lime village temple." Origins trace to a simple shrine on the kiln site in the mid-1800s, where workers far from home gathered to honour the Divine Mother.

In 1881, that devotion was given lasting form: Tamil labourers established Sri Veeramakaliamman Temple, dedicating it to Kali — the fierce, protecting face of Shakti who watches over her children.

Dravidian Architecture

Six hundred deities in stucco

The rajagopuram is a sacred map of the Hindu cosmos. Across its tiers climb some 600 figures — gods, goddesses, guardians and celestial beings — each hand-sculpted and painted, repainted with care at every re-consecration.

Within, the temple follows classical Dravidian planning: the sanctum of the Mother at its heart, encircled by the shrines of attendant deities, with halls for worship and community gathering.
